Before You Start

Word detection depends on your browser's speech recognition engine. Chrome and Edge on desktop give the best results. Browsers usually remove "um" and "uh" from the transcript, so Verba listens for those sounds directly in your microphone signal. That count is an estimate: it can miss short sounds or occasionally count a long, drawn-out word.

For the best experience, use Chrome on a desktop or laptop.

Your privacy: the MVP uses your microphone and your browser's built-in speech recognition. Verba does not record, save, or send your audio or transcript anywhere. Your browser provider (for example Google in Chrome, Microsoft in Edge, or Apple in Safari) may process the audio on its servers to convert it to text. Details are in our Privacy Policy.

    Voice profiles use voiceprints

    A voiceprint is a set of numbers that describes how a person sounds. It is biometric information, so Verba asks before making one.

    • Verba makes a voiceprint on this device from about 15 seconds of reading aloud, and keeps it only in this browser. It is never uploaded or shared.
    • It is used only to recognize who is speaking during practice on this device.
    • You can delete one voiceprint, or all voice data, at any time from the Voice profiles panel. Clearing this site’s data in your browser deletes it too.
    • Record someone’s voice only with their permission. For anyone under 13, a parent or guardian must agree first.

    Details are in our Privacy Policy.

    Pauses

    "Um" and "uh" are counted from the sound of your voice, because browsers usually leave them out of the transcript, and each one is marked in the transcript where it happened. That count is an estimate and is worked out on your device.

    Background noise detected. Verba has switched to its noise settings: only pauses of 1 second or longer are counted, and a sound has to be close to your speaking volume to count as an “um.”

    Verba is not receiving sound for its own analysis in this browser, so “um,” “uh,” and pauses can only be estimated from the transcript. Chrome or Edge on a computer gives the full result.

      Session type

      Verba needs about 3 seconds of speech to recognize a voice. It is most accurate in a quiet room and also works with background noise such as a fan or nearby conversation; in a very loud place it is less reliable. The first recording downloads Verba’s voice model (about 38 MB) from this site; it then works offline.

      Speaker model: WeSpeaker ResNet34-LM, trained on VoxCeleb, used under CC BY 4.0. Runs with ONNX Runtime Web (MIT).
