Before You Start

Word detection depends on your browser's speech recognition engine. Chrome and Edge on desktop give the best results. Browsers usually remove "um" and "uh" from the transcript, so Verba listens for those sounds directly in your microphone signal. That count is an estimate: it can miss short sounds or occasionally count a long, drawn-out word.

For the best experience, use Chrome on a desktop or laptop.

Your privacy: the demo uses your microphone and your browser's built-in speech recognition. Verba does not record, save, or send your audio or transcript anywhere. Your browser provider (for example Google in Chrome, Microsoft in Edge, or Apple in Safari) may process the audio on its servers to convert it to text. Details are in our Privacy Policy.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Everything you need to know about Verba.

    Is Verba free to use?
    The browser demo on this page is completely free with no account needed. Our full MVP is also free to try right now, and we do not collect any payment details. When the full product launches, we plan to charge $6 a month (USD) with a free tier. Any price, taxes, and renewal terms will be shown before you are asked to pay. See our Refund Policy.
    Does it work on my phone?
    Yes. The browser demo works on Android in Chrome and on iPhone in Safari or Chrome. For the best experience with the live demo we recommend Chrome on desktop or Android. The full MVP linked above runs in your phone's web browser. Vibration alerts work on Android; iPhone browsers do not allow web pages to vibrate.
    How is this different from just recording myself?
    Recording yourself means watching a video after the fact to catch your mistakes. Verba flags them while you are speaking: the screen flashes and, on Android, your phone vibrates when you say um, uh, or like. Immediate feedback is designed to help you notice the habit in the moment.
    Does Verba store my voice or speech data?
    Verba does not record, save, or send your audio or transcript when you use the on-page demo. The demo relies on your browser's built-in speech recognition, and some browsers (including Chrome and Edge) send audio to their provider's servers to convert it to text. The full MVP sends the recordings you submit to OpenAI for transcription and analysis. See our Privacy Policy for details, or make a data deletion request.
    What languages does Verba support?
    The browser demo currently supports English, Spanish, French, Mandarin, German, Japanese, Arabic, Hindi, Korean, Italian, and Russian. Select your language from the selector before starting your session and Verba will transcribe and detect filler words in that language.
    When will the full app launch?
    We don't have a fixed date yet. We're focused on building it right rather than building it fast. Join the newsletter above and you'll be the first to know.
    Can I track my own specific filler words?
    Yes. In the live demo there is a custom word tracker field where you can type any word you want Verba to detect. If you say “basically” or “honestly” or any other word constantly, type it in before starting your session and Verba will track it alongside um, uh, and like. After an English session, Verba also points out words you repeated unusually often, and one tap tracks that word next time.
    Does Verba keep a history of my practice?
    Only if you turn on progress tracking. Your session scores, pace, filler rate and pause counts are then saved in this browser on this device, so you can see your progress and your practice streak. Your words are never saved. Nothing is sent to Verba, and you can stop saving or delete your history at any time from the “Your progress” panel.
    Can I install Verba on my phone?
    Yes. On Android, open the site in Chrome and choose “Add to home screen” (or use the install button under the demo when it appears). On iPhone, open it in Safari, tap the Share button and choose “Add to Home Screen.” Verba then opens like an app. The speech transcript still needs an internet connection in most browsers.
    What do practice modes do?
    Pick a mode such as a 60-second pitch, an interview answer or a class presentation, and tap “Give me a topic” for a prompt. Timed modes count down and end the session automatically when time runs out, so you practice speaking to a limit.
