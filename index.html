Before You Start

Word detection depends on your browser's speech recognition engine. Chrome and Edge on desktop give the best results. Browsers usually remove "um" and "uh" from the transcript, so MicReady listens for those sounds directly in your microphone signal. That count is an estimate: it can miss short sounds or occasionally count a long, drawn-out word.

For the best experience, use Chrome on a desktop or laptop.

Your privacy: the demo uses your microphone and your browser's built-in speech recognition. MicReady does not record, save, or send your audio or transcript anywhere. Your browser provider (for example Google in Chrome, Microsoft in Edge, or Apple in Safari) may process the audio on its servers to convert it to text. Details are in our Privacy Policy.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Everything you need to know about MicReady.

    Is MicReady free to use?
    The lightweight demo on this page and the full MicReady MVP are both completely free with no account needed, and we do not collect any payment details. When the full product launches, we plan to charge $6 a month (USD) with a free tier. Any price, taxes, and renewal terms will be shown before you are asked to pay. See our Refund Policy.
    Does it work on my phone?
    Yes. The MVP works on Android in Chrome and on iPhone in Safari or Chrome. For the best experience we recommend Chrome on desktop or Android. Vibration alerts work on Android; iPhone browsers do not allow web pages to vibrate.
    How is this different from just recording myself?
    Recording yourself means watching a video after the fact to catch your mistakes. MicReady flags them while you are speaking: the screen flashes and, on Android, your phone vibrates when you say um or uh. Immediate feedback is designed to help you notice the habit in the moment.
    Does MicReady store my voice or speech data?
    MicReady does not record, save, or send your audio or transcript when you use the demo or the MVP. Both rely on your browser's built-in speech recognition, and some browsers (including Chrome and Edge) send audio to their provider's servers to convert it to text. See our Privacy Policy for details, or make a data deletion request.
    What languages does MicReady support?
    The MVP currently supports English, Spanish, French, Mandarin, German, Japanese, Arabic, Hindi, Korean, Italian, and Russian. Select your language from the selector before starting your session and MicReady will transcribe and detect filler words in that language.
    When will the full app launch?
    We don't have a fixed date yet. We're focused on building it right rather than building it fast. Join the newsletter above and you'll be the first to know.
    Which filler words does MicReady listen for?
    In English, MicReady counts “um” and “uh” from the sound of your voice and marks each one in the transcript. It does not count “like,” because most of the time it is a real word, as in “I like to eat.” Hedges such as “I think” and “kind of” are counted separately. The other ten languages each have their own common fillers and hesitation sounds. After an English session, MicReady also points out any words you repeated unusually often, such as “basically” or “honestly.”
    Does MicReady keep a history of my practice?
    Only if you turn on progress tracking in the MVP. Your session scores, pace, filler rate and pause counts are then saved in this browser on this device, so you can see your progress and your practice streak. Your words are never saved. Nothing is sent to MicReady, and you can stop saving or delete your history at any time from the “Your progress” panel.
    Can I install MicReady on my phone?
    Yes. On Android, open the site in Chrome and choose “Add to home screen” (or use the install button on the MVP page when it appears). On iPhone, open it in Safari, tap the Share button and choose “Add to Home Screen.” MicReady then opens like an app. The speech transcript still needs an internet connection in most browsers.
    What do practice modes do?
    In the MVP, pick a mode such as a 60-second pitch, an interview answer or a class presentation, and tap “Give me a topic” for a prompt. Timed modes count down and end the session automatically when time runs out, so you practice speaking to a limit.
    What are voice profiles?
    Voice profiles are an optional beta feature of the MVP. Each person reads a short passage aloud once, for about 15 seconds, and MicReady makes a voiceprint from it on your device. During practice, MicReady then recognizes who is speaking and keeps each person’s progress separate. In solo practice it ignores other voices nearby. In group practice, such as a team rehearsing a presentation, it follows who is speaking as people take turns and gives everyone their own score. Voiceprints are never uploaded, and you can delete them at any time. Recognition is most accurate in a quiet room and also works with moderate background noise; in a very loud place it is less reliable. See our Privacy Policy.
